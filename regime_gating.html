Gating Hypothesis

Does it help to let a strategy trade only in a particular market phase? Pilot with 25 strategies and 33 gate candidates: Model 1 gates on the BTC phase, Model 2 on the coin's own phase, Model 3 on both at once. The exits stay unchanged.

⚠
Result: not proven so far. For the 31 single-state candidates there is no reliable evidence that additional entry gating systematically improves the excess return: Model 1 improves 16 of 31, Model 2 nine of 31, Model 3 fourteen of 31, and none of them can be told apart from a coin flip. Models 1 and 3 cut a median 60 % of the trades. The reasoning is in the “Conclusion” section below. Models 1/2/3 — regime-specialist pilot

25 strategies, 33 gate candidates — top 10 per ADX state, LCB ranking

Model 0 is the natural, ungated trading behavior and is in the Regime specialists artifact. Models 1/2/3 add a pure entry gate, the exits stay unchanged: Model 1 gates on the BTC market phase, Model 2 on the coin's own regime, Model 3 on both at once with an AND (a long signal only passes if the BTC state and the coin state are both allowed). Replaces the earlier 7-strategy pilot completely since 2026-09-14 — that was not a curated selection but simply the first 7 strategies alphabetically, and was discarded at the owner's request (old result files remain as a historical trace but are no longer shown here).

ℹ
Four symmetric single-state gates instead of the old trend-following coupling. -uptrend/-downtrend/-sideways/-transition: long AND short are each allowed only in the named ADX state. Replaces the old -trend gate (long only in uptrend, short only in downtrend, one combined candidate) as the default, because its directional coupling points the wrong way for some strategies: two candidates with a real bear edge had 0% short trades in their entire history — a -trend gate would have blocked all of their bear activity, because its bear permission is restricted to short. -trend remains as an optional fifth gate, but only for candidates that are futures-capable AND actually have long and short trades (checked on the raw data, not on the futures/spot label alone) — two here: FastSupertrend_optim3_rsi_80 and FSampleStrategy. Full derivation including the DeepSeek-v4-pro cross-check in REGIME_AUDIT_PLAN.md.
ℹ
Selection: direct LCB ranking in the target regime, not the spread to the worst regime. A first draft would have sorted by the spread between best and worst regime — DeepSeek-v4-pro pointed out that this measures contrast rather than target-regime quality, and real data confirmed it: only 4 of 10 candidates overlapped between the spread ranking and the direct ranking, with the spread favoring candidates with only a mediocre edge but a catastrophic side regime. Instead: per ADX state, all candidates that are at VALIDATION tier in exactly that state, sorted directly by episode_excess_lcb there, only episode_excess_lcb > 0 (a statistically real edge), top 10, deduplicated to one candidate per strategy family (near-identical parameter variants reduced to the best-ranked representative).
⚠
ADX Uptrend has only a single specialist in the entire stock. Of 584 strategies (Model 0 as of 2026-09-15, on which this selection rests) only FastSupertrend_optim3_rsi_80 reaches a statistically real edge (episode_excess_lcb > 0) in ADX Uptrend — in line with the finding documented above that uptrend was by far the hardest regime against buy & hold. The uptrend table below therefore deliberately has only one strategy; it is not a display error. Recalculated on 2026-09-20 (687 strategies): 0 have an edge in ADX Uptrend with episode_excess_lcb > 0: none. The selection for Models 1-3 stays as of 15.09., because the gated runs are based on it.
ℹ
Episode excess LCB / grade, here too. The same metric as in the Regime specialists artifact (full definition and derivation in its legend) — a 95% lower bound on this strategy's mean episodic excess return in this phase, not merely its bare mean. With few or strongly fluctuating episodes it falls well below the excess-return value itself; below 2 episodes no value appears. Grade is the corresponding A-F grade from the same fixed thresholds. Purely descriptive, no substitute for tier or excess-return ranking.
ℹ
Only what was actually gated on is shown. BTC and coin regime are both always measured for every trade, but Model 1 gates only on the BTC regime (hence only that table below), Model 2 only on the coin regime (hence only that one). Model 3 gates on both at once (BTC AND coin), hence one combined table instead of two separate BTC/coin breakdowns — benchmarked against the real intersection of the BTC episode and the coin episode, not against either single episode. Because of the symmetric single-state gate, most candidates have at most one possible phase result (consistency therefore 0% or 100%); only the two -trend candidates can reach two phase results (uptrend/downtrend) and thus also 50% consistency.

ℹ
Model 2 structurally brings almost nothing — nearly tautological, not chance. 4 of 31 candidates are bit-for-bit identical to Model 0 (same trade count, same excess return). Reason: the candidates were selected in the first place for having a statistically real edge in exactly this coin-regime state (episode excess LCB ranking, see above) — a gate on the same dimension that was already selected on can hardly change anything. This is a real, informative finding about this selection method, but not a general statement that “coin gating never helps”: this comparison tests the benefit of a coin gate for candidates already selected on coin regime, not for unselected strategies.
ℹ
Models 1/3 cut half the trades on average without demonstrable gain. BTC regime and coin regime are only moderately correlated — measured rather than merely assumed: regime_daily.csv, 18,000 day/pair rows, agreement rate 53%, Cohen's kappa 0.38 (“fair” by Landis/Koch, well below “high”). A gate on the more weakly related BTC dimension therefore throws away usable trades without the extra condition adding any visible value — the result is close to a coin flip, neither direction (16/31 and 14/31 improved) is statistically distinguishable from 50%.
⚠
Limits of this statement — checked by DeepSeek-v4-pro, adopted here. With n=31, “16 of 31 improved” is not significantly different from 50% by a binomial test — the correct reading is “no significant effect demonstrable”, not “no effect”. Means are outlier-prone (with 2–8 trades after the gate a single trade moves the figure): the FreqForge score delta is ≈0 at the median for all three models, but +6 points at the mean for Models 1/3 because of a few outliers, with the IQR running from −2 to +8. The 31 candidates are also not independent (25 base strategies, some with several target regimes, e.g. cryptotank) — this reduces the effective sample size further. And: the candidates are already pre-filtered by the selection criterion on a coin-regime edge, so the results cannot be generalized to arbitrary strategies without checking.
ℹ
Conclusion. For these 31 already regime-selected specialists there is no reliable evidence that additional entry gating — on whichever dimension — systematically improves the results. The individual cases with a visible improvement lie in the noise of a sample that is too small, not in a robust pattern, at a noticeable cost (up to 60% fewer trades, a third of the candidates end up below 10 trades).

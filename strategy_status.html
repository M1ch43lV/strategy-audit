Strategy Test Bench

What is known today about each of the 900 Freqtrade strategies: which cleared every gate, which did not, and exactly where each one stopped.

Distribution

443 strategies are usable today \u2014 the 67 of the frozen baseline plus 376 admitted since. As of 2026-09-02 a row is admitted when it clears both bias gates at a warm-up that settles its indicators: look-ahead PASS measured here, recursion settled by the ladder, coverage PASS, trap-free. The preregistration previously routed a settled row whose trade list changed under that warm-up to exploratory rather than admitted; that requirement is retired, because the aim is the mathematically clean result under the current freqtrade rather than a reproduction of what an author once ran. Fewer trades, or more, is the consequence of measuring properly.

What that costs, stated plainly. The retired paired run was the only test separating a repaired row from a reconfigured one. Of the 26 it had already decided, 13 produced an identical trade list and 13 did not \u2014 SlowPotato 1,835 against 1,899, JuicyTrend 13,698 against 13,607. So a market-phase number here cannot be assumed to be what the author's own configuration would have produced. Every admitted row therefore records the warm-up it was measured at and whether that value is at or below the author's own: 2 needed no supplied warm-up, 126 declare none at all, and 226 declare one that runs and was overridden.

Nothing here is untested, and nothing here is cleared by the original sweep. The audit's first pass ran in an environment that did not establish the preconditions this one requires, so its verdicts are provenance and hints, never evidence. Rows marked inherited are admitted on a look-ahead verdict from that sweep and are queued for native re-measurement.

The order the checks run in

The order is not arbitrary: each step needs what the one before it produces.

1 · Trial run

One month over eight pairs: does the strategy start, and does it trade. A strategy that fails here is open, never excluded — no check has seen it, so nothing about it has been judged. It is labelled to be fixed until the obstacle is either removed or shown to be the strategy's own. repair_verdict then says which it is, and that is a separate question from whether the strategy is still in play.

2 · Recursion, on the warm-up ladder

Second because it needs no trades — it compares indicator values, not signals — so it can judge a strategy the look-ahead check cannot yet touch. It produces the warm-up at which the indicators settle, which the next step needs.

3 · Look-ahead, at that warm-up

Freqtrade's lookahead-analysis builds a Backtesting object, and Backtesting.__init__ takes required_startup from the strategy's declared startup_candle_count. So the check runs at whatever warm-up is in force — and 125 strategies declare none, which would have their signals compared on indicators still undefined at the start of the window. Running it after the ladder means running it at a value shown to settle them. This check needs ten trades, and widens its window rather than failing when there are fewer.

4 · Backtest over the full window

Only for a strategy that has cleared both bias checks: 20200301-20260821, six and a half years over all eight pairs, at the warm-up the ladder settled on. Two runs of about four minutes each per strategy, so it is the most expensive step by a wide margin — which is why it comes last and only for strategies whose numbers can be trusted. Admission follows from it, and the market-phase work is built on it.

What excludes a strategy

Three things, and nothing else. Each is a result this audit produced itself, on this data, in this runtime.

C1 · Look-ahead found

lookahead = FOUND from a run of ours. The strategy reads data it could not have had at the time, and no repair undoes that without rewriting what the author wrote.

C2 · Recursion found

recursive_evidence = convergence:not_settled. The ladder supplied up to a year of warm-up and an indicator still moved by 1 % or more at every rung, so its value depends on where the backtest starts.

C3 · Never trades

Ran 20200301-20260821 over all eight pairs and opened nothing. There is nothing to classify by market phase. Only when nothing on our side stopped it — four of the eleven turned out to be our setup, not the strategy.

A strategy satisfying none of these is not excluded. It is unfinished, and open_work says what is missing. Five things have at one time or another excluded strategies here and have been withdrawn: the source-code trap heuristic, a verdict inherited from the original sweep, an NA, the analyzer refusing for want of a warm-up, and a failed trial run. Three of them had removed strategies from the work before anyone looked.

The criteria live in exclusion_criteria_list.md and the repair routes in repair_measures_list.md. Both are generated with this page from the same rows, and the generator refuses a row excluded for a reason not written down, or a repair route taken and not recorded.

One pair for the bias checks is freqtrade's own doing, not a choice of this audit: recursive-analysis logs “Using pair BTC/USDT only for recursive analysis. Replacing whitelist.” and replaces whatever the config holds.

The look-ahead check widens its window rather than failing. It needs ten trades; when the frozen window yields fewer it retries on the first fallback and then the second, and the record keeps every window it tried. A verdict from a wider window is still that strategy's verdict, but it was not reached over the same span as its neighbour's.

The trial run answers one question: does the strategy start and trade. One month over eight pairs is enough for that and cheap. What it earns is measured later, over the full window.

The warm-up ladder steps in days — 1, 2, 7, 14, 30, 90, 365 — converted to each strategy's own timeframe, and accepts a rung once every indicator stays inside 1.0 %.

A convergence candidate has cleared both gates, not merely failed neither. 35 rows were candidates on a look-ahead NA — the gate ran and returned nothing, for eleven different reasons — and each was queued for the paired full-window run: hours of computation for a row that could not be admitted whatever that run showed. They are held now until the gate is re-measured, and the paired queue drops from 389 to 354.

A finding at either gate is disqualifying, and the table cannot say otherwise. The selftest refuses to let a row with a look-ahead or recursion FOUND sit among the admitted or the convergence candidates, and a recursion finding the ladder confirmed \u2014 every rung run, the drift still there \u2014 must be an excluded row, never an open one. NA is a different thing entirely: the gate ran and returned nothing, and the reason is printed under the row.

Click a column header to sort. A gate reads – when there is no verdict bound to this implementation: either it has not been re-measured, or the only verdict available comes from the original sweep. Hover any cell for the detail.

The 152 rows that never ran, and what has become of them. Freqtrade reports most load failures as Impossible to load Strategy 'X', which names none of the three things it could be, so each file was imported directly in the pinned runtime and the real exception recorded. Two repair routes have run since. 40 are repaired and now produce measurements — 38 declared their timeframe under freqtrade's pre-2021.4 name, 2 needed only their own helper module put back on the import path. 6 were repaired past their original obstacle and now fail on something else, which is its own message rather than a mask. 4 repairs were withdrawn: the only corpus copy of their helper sits in a directory carrying its own freqtrade/, which shadows the installed package. 28 remain to be fixed, 59 need a look — chiefly 20 importing a third-party package absent from the pinned image — and 15 are refused, either because the strategy declares no stoploss or no exit logic, or because no timeframe can be recovered without inventing one. The full list is REPAIR_LIST.md.

Excluded is a verdict, and it is not issued on somebody else's measurement or on the absence of one. Going through all 377 rows that carried it: 110 rest on a disqualifying result measured here from that implementation, and 152 never ran at all, so nothing about them was judged. The other 115 — 43 on a verdict inherited from the original sweep, 72 on no disqualifying result whatever — have been moved to exclusion unconfirmed and are held there until a measurement of ours settles them. Nothing is hidden by the change of name: the decisive reason and the badge stay on the row, and the work that would settle it is queued. The selftest now fails if anything reaches excluded on borrowed or absent evidence.

This is not a softening. Many held rows will end up excluded — the 38 on an inherited look-ahead finding probably will, since a limited environment does not invent bias. They will end up there on our own evidence or not at all.

Three things the reader got wrong, and what each cost. The analyzer prints one column per warm-up and sorts them by value, so the strategy's own column moves; reading the first numeric column instead measured drift at somebody else's warm-up. It printed nan where an indicator was not yet defined, and those cells were skipped, which turned "nothing could be compared" into "nothing deviated". And when every indicator matches the full-history run exactly it prints no table at all, just a sentence saying so — which was read as a missing measurement rather than as the strongest available pass. Each was found by re-reading stored logs, and each is now a rule with a test behind it.

Where a log survived, it has been read again. Of 124 recursion records with their log, 55 said something other than what this table showed: 40 have no verdict at all, one is clean, and 14 keep their verdict with corrected numbers. Seventeen ladder runs recorded as inconclusive were in fact clean at the first rung. What each replaced is kept on the record. The 76 records that kept no log cannot be checked and stay queued.

Recursion shows the current state, not the original one. PASS @ 14d means no indicator drifts once fourteen days of warm-up are supplied, so the earlier exclusion was a measurement question rather than a property of the strategy. PASS* marks the few rows that only meet the 1 % band and not the original 0.01 %.

Every row carries the commands it was produced by — one per gate. bt is the backtest, la the look-ahead analysis, rec the recursion analysis. They are listed separately because they differ in more than their subcommand: the bias gates run against a config that forces price_side=other, since look-ahead analysis forces market orders and freqtrade will not evaluate a signal without it, while a backtest uses the plain config and adds --fee 0.001 --export trades --cache none. A single column could only ever have shown one of the three. Hover any line for the full command.

A repaired row carries what a later run has to reproduce. Its settings line names the rules, the config, the model and the environment the repair needs — the parts that cannot appear on a command line, such as PYTHONPATH additions or PROFILE_COMPAT_SIGNATURES, and the recovered timeframe that lives in a generated config nobody could guess. The reconstructed gate commands are built from those same facts and point at user_data/profile_bias_strategies/<strategy>, the directory holding that strategy alone: freqtrade's resolver imports every file beside the one it wants, and a neighbour that raises at import time takes the run with it. The table's selftest fails if a repaired row states neither its settings nor a recorded call.

A plain line was recorded as it ran; one with an amber rule is reconstructed from the run profile and window, because nothing stored the call before 2026-09-01 — a reconstruction is a different claim from a recording. The full-window backtest is eight calls, one per pair; the cell leaves the pair as a placeholder and states the count, while every individual call with its own console output is appended to user_data/freqtrade_runs.log.

A row that failed here is not a row that never ran. 152 strategies were attempted in this runtime, on the date shown, and stopped with an error; the message is under the reason. Forty-eight of them are blocked on nothing but a missing timeframe declaration.

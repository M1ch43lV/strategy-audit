Zaratustra - 32 row(s) in the corpus

One comparison decides everything: while RSI(7) sits above RSI(14) the strategy is long, while it sits below it is short, and each side is closed as soon as the two cross back. There is no other filter and no signal-per-candle logic - the position is a state of the RSI spread. Exits in practice come from the risk settings, which are deliberately tight: 1% stoploss, 0.1% trailing stop and a flat 1% take-profit.

Caveat: The site's own family table advertises this family as 10x with a trailing stop. This revision's leverage() returns 1, so the published description and the code in the corpus disagree about position size - the description there may belong to a different revision or to a bot configuration.

Slope - 7 row(s) in the corpus

A pure swing-point reversal: scipy.signal.argrelextrema marks the local minima and maxima of the close over a rolling 64-bar window, a fresh local minimum opens a long and a fresh local maximum closes it, with the short side mirrored. There is no stoploss and no profit target in the usual sense - stoploss -0.999 and minimal_roi 1 mean the trade only ends when the opposite extreme appears.

A 5m scalper whose entry is a bundle of conditions ORed together rather than one rule: EMA(8/16) against EMA/SMA(15) and RSI(4/14/20), the stochastic-fast fastk, and a Bollinger band around the typical price are collected into a list and reduced with `lambda x, y: x | y`, so any single member can open a trade.

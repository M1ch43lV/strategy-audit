Worst trade / Sortino / return p.a. / profit factor / FreqForge score, in every table on this page. All five are raw or point values from exactly this regime, not over the strategy's whole history. Profit factor = gross profit ÷ gross loss (∞ with zero losing trades — per the FreqForge convention the best case, not undefined; the same convention in every table on this page since 2026-09-14, before which another internal table deviated with NaN). Sortino shows "no loss" instead of a number when there was simply not a single losing trade in this regime (Freqtrade's own “-100” sentinel convention for a metric without downside dispersion — FreqForge treats that as the best case, not as an error). Return p.a. is a linear, non-compounding annual extrapolation (mean trade return × trades per year) — deliberately not a CAGR: with the fixed-$1000-per-trade bookkeeping used here (every trade its own fresh stake, nothing reinvested) a true compound-interest formula would explode astronomically at high trade frequency without saying anything about actual strategy quality (correction 2026-09-14, after a DeepSeek-v4-pro review of the whole metric code). Sortino is likewise annualised at trade level (square root of trades per year instead of the earlier dimensionally inconsistent mix of daily and per-trade metrics) and can still become large at very high trading frequency together with return p.a. — from 1,000 upward it is shown compactly with a K/M/B suffix, which is not rounding to a smaller number. The FreqForge score is a weighted 0-100 point value modelled on the six categories of github.com/baxr6/FreqForge: Sortino 25%, drawdown control 25%, return p.a. 15%, liquidation safety 15%, profit factor 10%, worst-trade severity 10%. Purely additional report columns, not a ranking criterion — the "no single score" rule from REGIME_AUDIT_PLAN.md §17 still applies to tier and excess-return ranking. Liquidation safety feeds into the score but has been narrowed since 2026-09-14 to genuine forced liquidations (exit_reason == "liquidation") instead of also counting harmless force_exit exits (e.g. end of the backtest window) — as a result it sits at 100 points for practically every strategy (only {{n_forced_exits}} of {{trades_mio}} million trades in the whole inventory are forced exits of any kind).
ℹ
Episode excess LCB / grade, in every table on this page — deliberately separate from the FreqForge score. LCB stands for lower confidence bound. It is a value below which the true mean excess return does not fall with 95 % confidence. A strategy's mean is only an estimate from a limited number of episodes; the lower bound says how much of it can still be believed in the unfavourable case. If it is above 0, the lead over buy-and-hold is statistically reliable. It answers directly "does trading beat B&H, AND is that statistically reliable (many episodes), or just luckily good on a few?" Calculation, agreed with DeepSeek-v4-pro: for each independent episode the strategy return minus the buy-and-hold return of that one episode is formed, from that the mean and dispersion over all episodes, then the one-sided 95% lower bound LCB = mean − t(0.95, n−1) × standard error. Few or strongly fluctuating episodes automatically pull the bound deep into the negative — without a separate minimum-trade rule, the reliability is already in the formula. Grade is a Gainium-style A-F grade from fixed thresholds on the same LCB value (A: >+2%, B: 0 to +2%, C: −2% to 0, D: −5% to −2%, F: below) — fixed before any results were seen, not chosen afterwards to fit. With fewer than 2 episodes no value appears (EXPLORATORY strategies with 0-1 episodes in a phase cannot sensibly be bounded). Likewise a purely additional column, no substitute for tier or excess-return ranking. {{callout_robust}}

Ranking rule

SplitDiscovery 2020–03 to 2023–12 · validation from 2024–01. Ranking happens in the validation window only.
Specialist floor≥5 episodes AND ≥10 trades in the same regime, otherwise tier EXPLORATORY — reported, never ranked.
BenchmarkThe coin's own spot buy&hold over the whole ADX regime episode (first to last classified day), not only over the open–close interval of the single trade (merge_asof against 1-minute candles; until 2026-09-11 it was the trade interval — see the callout below).
MetricExcess return = mean episode return minus mean benchmark return — each episode is first summed on its own, and only then averaged over the episodes, never over single trades (until 2026-09-13 it was the latter — see the correction below). No single-value score, no selection by best result.
Median excess returnMedian of a strategy's own excess-return values over the market phases in which it reaches the floor (up to 4: BULL/BEAR/SIDEWAYS/TRANSITION or the four ADX states) — one level above excess return itself, not over trades or episodes. A median over only 2-4 values is simply the middle regime value (with 4 values: the average of the two middle ones) — more robust against a single outlier regime than the mean over all four, but with so few values no substitute for a real distribution.
Dollar viewFixed $1000 stake per trade, no reinvestment, plain sum — see the section below for the rejected alternative.

ℹ
Why there is a split between discovery and validation.

The problem. Hundreds of strategies are compared here across four market phases and two regime kinds (BTC and coin), so thousands of combinations. With so many comparisons, some always shine by chance. Anyone who finds a strategy for a market phase on the same data on which they evaluate it almost inevitably overrates it (data snooping, multiple testing).

The solution. The data is split in time into two windows: discovery (2020-03 to 2023-12) and validation (2024-01 to 2026-08). Both are evaluated separately with the same evaluation and the same thresholds. The rankings on this page rest on the validation alone; nothing from discovery flows into rank, tier or selection. Its purpose is the counter-test in the next section: does what proved itself in discovery hold in validation? The boundary is a calendar date frozen on 2026-09-11. It was checked only against episode counts, never against strategy results.

Do not confuse. EXPLORATORY is a tier (a strategy below the specialist floor in a market phase), not a time window. Discovery and validation are the two time windows.

What follows. Rank, tier and specialist floor apply only in the validation window; the episodes for the floor are also counted only there (correction of 2026-09-14). The cost stress of stage 8b uses the same window. The thresholds of 5 episodes and 10 trades were set against the episode counts of this window (BTC: 15 to 36 episodes per phase).

Why by calendar and not at random. A market phase is a contiguous episode of days to months. If trades were distributed at random over both halves, the same episode would lie in both, and the validation would no longer be an independent test.

Limits. (1) There is one boundary and a validation window of about 2.7 years; the second half is also a different market situation from the first, so a finding that does not hold there may be due to the strategy or to the market. (2) Until the comparison below, discovery was not evaluated: it made no selection and produced no ranking. (3) The validation is not a fully locked hold-out: the candidates for models 1 to 3 were chosen from the validation results of model 0 (see the section further below), so their gated results in the same window are not an independent test.

Discovery vs. validation: does what proved itself hold?

the same evaluation on both time windows, strategy by strategy

For each strategy and each market phase, the excess return is computed once from the trades of discovery (before 2024-01-01) and once from those of validation, with the same formula, the same benchmark and the same floor (at least 5 episodes and 10 trades in the respective window). The strategies compared are those that reach the floor in the respective market phase in both windows. Nothing from discovery flows into the rankings; this is a counter-test, not a selection.

The columns explained.

“Better than B&H” (formerly “positive”) means: extra gain over buy-and-hold, i.e. excess return above 0. It does not mean “agrees”. A strategy's excess return in a market phase is its return in the episodes of that phase minus the buy-and-hold return over the same episodes.

Discovery better than B&H → validation also better. Take all strategies that beat buy-and-hold in this phase in discovery and count how many of them do so again in validation. 81 % means: four out of five repeated it. The number in brackets is the size of the group; with 5 or 16 strategies the percentage says little.

Discovery not better → validation better. The same for the remaining strategies: how many were not better in discovery and became so in validation? A low value here alongside a high value on the left means: whoever did not convince in discovery mostly did not later either.

Base rate. The share of all compared strategies of this phase that are better than buy-and-hold in validation, without knowing discovery. It is the reference line: only what lies clearly above it shows persistence. In falling phases it is high, because there almost every long trade loses less than holding. Confirmation as a column: flag and score

ℹ

Flag “confirmed”. The strategy reaches the floor in the market phase in both windows, and the lower 95 % confidence bound (LCB, lower confidence bound, see the explanation above) of its excess return is above 0 in discovery and in validation. Whoever meets that was reliably better than buy-and-hold in both windows. This rarely happens by chance.

Score. The smaller of the two bounds, i.e. the weakest link. It is only high if the strategy is good in both windows. Similarly bad results earn nothing: they give a negative score and no flag. The score changes no ranking; it stands as its own column next to robustness and can be sorted.

Universal candidates. Strictly: confirmed in all four coin phases. If that does not hold, the milder rule applies: better than buy-and-hold in both windows in at least three of the four phases (excess above 0, floor in both). The strongest rule that applies is shown. A universal candidate's score is that of its weakest phase; whoever is only mildly confirmed therefore has a negative score, because at least one phase is not statistically reliable.

Status of the rule. Both windows were known when it was set, and the milder universal rule was introduced because the strict one left not a single candidate. The rule was therefore chosen after the fact and does not prove predictive power. It has been frozen since 2026-09-20 (addendum in REGIME_PREREGISTRATION.md) and is to be tested on data from 2026-08-21 onward, which have not yet been used for it.

⚠
How to read this. The persistence is high, but it does not prove phase skill. Excess return measures against buy-and-hold of the whole phase. A strategy that is rarely in the market therefore almost always beats the benchmark in falling phases and loses in rising phases, in both windows alike. Part of the rank correlation is thus trading style (time in market, frequency, leverage), and that stays the same for years. Also, these are the same strategies in both windows, not independent samples, and validation is a different market (2024 to 2026). Where the correlation is low, as in BTC sideways, persistence really is missing.

Phase portfolio: one strategy per market phase

Goal: at least {{daily_target}} return per day on the capital

The question: which strategy should run in which ADX phase of the coin so that the capital earns at least {{daily_target}} per day on average and buy-and-hold is beaten in that phase? The selection is a rule, not handwork: robustness PASS in the phase, confirmed in both windows (LCB above 0), a daily return of at least {{daily_target}} after 0.1 % slippage per side and above that of buy-and-hold, at least {{pf_min_trades}} trades. In the upward phase it stays with buy-and-hold; that is the owner's decision and at the same time the result, because no strategy meets the rule there.

ℹ
Two readings of the daily return. Both use a fixed stake per trade, without compounding.

On the capital deployed: sum of the trade returns divided by the capital days the trades were actually held (at least one hour per trade). Capital that does nothing does not count. A strategy that is in the market for only minutes easily reaches large numbers here; this reading flatters short trades.

On the capital provided: the same sum divided by the days of the phase, one slot per coin. Idle capital counts as well. This is what an account earns per day and unit of capital in this phase, and the stricter figure. The table uses this one. Buy-and-hold is always fully invested, so both are the same there.

ℹ
What the percentage measures (since 2026-09-12: per market phase, no longer per trade). The benchmark is a simple spot buy-and-hold of the same coin over the whole ADX-defined regime episode — from the first to the last day this regime applied — regardless of when exactly within that phase the strategy entered and exited. The percentage shown is strategy return minus this phase buy-and-hold return, where each episode is first summed on its own and only then averaged over the episodes (not over single trades — since 2026-09-13, see the correction further below). A pure 1x long strategy can thus beat the benchmark even without leverage and without shorting: for example by running several smaller trades instead of holding once through the whole phase, skipping pullbacks within the phase or repeatedly entering cheaper and exiting dearer. (Until 2026-09-11 the benchmark was instead limited to the trade's own duration — there an unleveraged long could practically never reach more than 0%, see the amendment history.)

ADX regime: definition and periods

how the four states arise, and when each applied to each of the 8 coins

All four states — whether for BTC or for a single coin — arise from the same rule applied to daily candles: Wilder DMI/ADX(14) (talib.PLUS_DI/MINUS_DI/ADX, period 14). Each day is assigned the state that the candle completed on the previous day produced (a full day of delay) — no look into the future.

ADX uptrendADX ≥ 25 AND +DI > −DI — strong, directed upward trend.
ADX downtrendADX ≥ 25 AND −DI > +DI — strong, directed downward trend.
ADX sidewaysADX < 20, regardless of +DI/−DI — no pronounced trend in either direction.
ADX transition20 ≤ ADX < 25 — transition zone between sideways and a pronounced trend, direction not yet confirmed.
ℹ
Coin regime is not an 8-coin vote — each coin gets its own independent state. The 8 pairs (BTC, ETH, LTC, XRP, ADA, XLM, XMR, DASH, each against USDT) run through exactly the same rule above, but each on its own daily candles — not as a majority decision or average over all 8. "Coin regime" in the tables on this page is always the state of the one coin on which the respective trade ran; ADA can show ADX uptrend on a day when BTC shows ADX sideways on the same day. "BTC regime" is the same mechanism, only computed for BTC itself and then assigned to all 8 pairs on that day as a shared market context (hence it is identical for all pairs on a day, coin regime is not). Separately, regime_daily.csv also holds breadth metrics (pct_coins_bull_state and the like, what percentage of the 8 coins are currently in the same state) — these feed into no table on this page, they are pure additional context.

Historical: why dollar gain > B&H gain could occur despite a negative excess return — and why that no longer happens. Until the correction below (2026-09-14), excess return averaged over trades, while dollar gain summed over trades and B&H gain only over episodes — two different counting bases. Example at the time: FastSupertrend_optim3_rsi_80 had 1,536 trades over only 39 episodes; the trade mean (+0.61%) was below B&H's episode mean (+7.80%), although the dollar sum of the trades ($9,435) exceeded that of the episodes ($8,188) — because here simply 39 times more single amounts were summed. Since excess return is also averaged per episode, this contradiction has disappeared: FastSupertrend_optim3_rsi_80 itself now shows +1.19% excess return, matching the higher dollar gain, and in the whole model 0 inventory ({{tbl_btc}} combinations of strategy and BTC phase, {{tbl_coin}} of strategy and coin phase, as of 2026-09-20) the old constellation (dollar gain higher, but excess return negative) occurs {{contradiction_text}}.

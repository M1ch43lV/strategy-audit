Distribution

The order the checks run in

The order is not arbitrary: each step needs what the one before it produces.

1 · Trial run

One month over eight pairs, widened to three months and then one year while fewer than ten trades are observed: does the strategy start, and does it trade. A strategy that fails here is open, never excluded — no check has seen it, so nothing about it has been judged. It is labelled to be fixed until the obstacle is either removed or shown to be the strategy's own. repair_verdict then says which it is, and that is a separate question from whether the strategy is still in play.

2 · Look-ahead

Second because the recursion gate will not spend a run without it: profile_bias.py's recursive_prerequisite() returns “Look-Ahead is not PASS” and defers, since a look-ahead finding is not something a warm-up can repair. Freqtrade's lookahead-analysis builds a Backtesting object, and Backtesting.__init__ takes required_startup from the strategy's declared startup_candle_count — so this check runs at whatever the author declared, and 125 strategies declare nothing at all, which compares their signals on indicators still undefined at the start of the window. That is a known weakness of this check, recorded per row rather than argued away: the settled warm-up the next step produces is not available yet here. The check needs ten trades, and widens its window rather than failing when there are fewer.

3 · Recursion, on the warm-up ladder

Only for a strategy whose look-ahead reads PASS. It needs no trades — it compares indicator values, not signals — and it climbs every rung the data allows to find the warm-up at which the indicators stop moving. That settled warm-up is what the full backtest then runs at, so this step produces something the next one needs, which is why it sits here and not last.

4 · Backtest over the full window

Only for a strategy that has cleared both bias checks: 20200301-20260821, six and a half years over all eight pairs, at the warm-up the ladder settled on. Two runs of about four minutes each per strategy, so it is the most expensive step by a wide margin — which is why it comes last and only for strategies whose numbers can be trusted. Admission follows from it, and the market-phase work is built on it.

__CRITERIA__

A strategy satisfying none of these is not excluded. It is unfinished, and open_work says what is missing. __NOT_CRITERIA_COUNT__ things have at one time or another excluded strategies here and have been withdrawn: the source-code trap heuristic, a verdict inherited from the original sweep, an NA, the analyzer refusing for want of a warm-up, and a failed trial run. The criteria live in exclusion_criteria_list.md and the repair routes in repair_measures_list.md. Both are generated with this page from the same rows, and the generator refuses a row excluded for a reason not written down, or a repair route taken and not recorded.

The eight pairs against USDT are BTC, ETH, LTC, XRP, ADA, XLM, XMR and DASH, at a 0.1 % fee with max_open_trades=8.

One pair for the bias checks is freqtrade's own doing, not a choice of this audit: recursive-analysis logs “Using pair BTC/USDT only for recursive analysis. Replacing whitelist.” and replaces whatever the config holds.

The look-ahead check widens its window rather than failing. It needs ten trades; when the frozen window yields fewer it retries on the first fallback and then the second, and the record keeps every window it tried. A verdict from a wider window is still that strategy's verdict, but it was not reached over the same span as its neighbour's.

The trial run answers one question: does the strategy start and trade. One month over eight pairs is enough for that and cheap. What it earns is measured later, over the full window.

The warm-up ladder steps in days — 1, 2, 7, 14, 30, 90, 365 — converted to each strategy's own timeframe, and accepts a rung once every indicator stays inside 1.0 %.
